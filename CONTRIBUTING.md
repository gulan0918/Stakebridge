# Contributing to StakeBridge

Help another team understand whom to engage, why the perspective matters, what to ask and how feedback can inform a project. Useful contributions include constraints, unsuccessful approaches and limits, as well as positive outcomes.

## Propose an entry

Use the app's **Contribute** page to prepare a proposal. Download the JSON file, open the linked GitHub issue form and attach it. You can also open an **Entry proposal** issue directly. GitHub handles contributor sign-in; the app does not collect GitHub credentials.

Propose a documented person, organisation, interview group or anonymised perspective. Include the name, role, product stages, domain, contributing team, public source, rationale, interview account, integration evidence and limitations. Add links to published materials and retain dates as recorded. Identify completed actions and future plans clearly.

Maintainers assign the permanent ID. Do not reuse another entry's ID for a new person or guide.

## Add experience or request a correction

Use the experience or correction link on the entry's detail page. Include its `SB_` ID, your team and year, the context, what you tried, what changed and a public evidence link. Submit your own account rather than rewriting the original team's experience.

Corrections can also request removal of information that should not be public. Do not repeat private information in the issue: identify the entry and describe the problem without disclosing it again.

## Contact permission

Published professional contact details may be included when an official university or institutional source makes them public. Include that source, label general institutional channels correctly and record the verification date. Do not guess identities from similar names.

A past interview or a public address does not establish willingness to receive new interviews. An “open to interviews” status requires all of the following:

1. An explicitly authorised public HTTPS contact channel.
2. A public source documenting that permission.
3. A permission expiry date.

Do not submit patient identities, private email addresses, phone numbers, medical records, unpublished recordings or confidential material. Role-based and anonymised experience is welcome. An entry can be useful with `availability: "unrecorded"` and no public contact channel.

## Review and publication

An issue is a proposal. A maintainer checks attribution, evidence, privacy, contact permission and data structure, and prepares a pull request. The published registry changes only after review, merge and a successful deployment. A passing validation check does not replace a person's review of the evidence.

Registry text contributions are published under **CC BY 4.0**; code contributions use **MIT**. Only contribute material you have the rights and permission to publish under those terms.
